# Ryukyu origin anchor — source-audited hypothesis handoff (2026-10-17)

## Why this belongs in anto
The visible white-vs-bluish-purple contrast across the Ryukyu *Cirsium brevicaule* / *C. irumtiense* complex motivates the question of **plant-dispersal history and the geographical transport of latent floral capacity**. It does **not** establish a white ancestor, repeated floral reacquisition, or direction of dispersal.

## New empirical anchor — Chang et al. (2026)
[Chang et al. 2026, BMC Plant Biology, DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-08097-6) used phylotranscriptomic data from 37 accessions including outgroups and multiple *Cirsium* samples. The authors:
- distinguish reciprocally monophyletic *C. brevicaule* and *C. irumtiense* lineages across the Miyako Strait;
- report white corollas for *C. brevicaule* and bluish-purple for *C. irumtiense*;
- estimate their **lineage split** at ~0.93 Ma (95% HPD ~0.71–1.33 Ma);
- report markedly divergent nuclear genome sizes (2C ~1.93 pg vs ~0.69 pg);
- provide raw-read BioProject **PRJNA1311153** (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA1311153).

**Important taxonomic conflict:** Kew Plants of the World Online currently includes *C. irumtiense* under accepted *C. brevicaule* (https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:195099-1). Treat species delimitation as a competing, dated taxonomy concept, not as a silently resolved fact. This newer study supports separate evolutionary units but does not universally change nomenclatural treatment.

**Scope ceiling:** lineage split ≠ pigmentation-loss date ≠ regulatory-reactivation date. Differences in 2C do not alone establish a particular cytotype or ploidy mechanism. The source's young-leaf RNA sequencing does not demonstrate flower-specific regulatory reactivation. Published evidence of limited recent gene flow is not evidence of zero historical introgression at pigment loci.

## Primary competing histories (all open)
- H1: ancestral coloured state; pigment expression lost in one regional descendant; southern pigment retained.
- H2: ancestral nonexpressing state; southern descendant independently reactivates existing floral pathway.
- H3: expression/pigment alleles acquired by introgression or admixture.
- H4: environment- or developmental-stage dependent pigmentation rather than a stable genetic transition.
- H5: unrecognized ancestral variation and lineage sorting, producing historical mosaics without a single clean colour polarization.

## Required discriminatory evidence
1. Sample multiple **independent populations**, with within-population morph contrasts wherever actual variation is confirmed. Preserve taxon-concept labels and locality privacy.
2. Use the **same individual** for calibrated corolla colour, anthocyanin chemistry, flower-specific transcriptome or targeted expression, leaf DNA, genome size and a voucher.
3. Recover nuclear ancestry and pigment-associated local genealogies; explicitly contrast genome-wide tree/ancestry with local haplotypes. Permit incomplete lineage sorting in nulls.
4. Compare floral tissue to other organs for pathway retention, but do not equate coding-gene presence with floral competence.
5. Validate heredity and inducibility in a common garden, controlled crosses or perturbation where permissions, feasibility and taxonomy allow.
6. Use dated-tree uncertainty rather than treating the 0.93 Ma split as a point estimate for an unknown colour event.

## Formal interpretation gates
G0: taxon and population concept clear, material legitimately accessible. No field authorization implied.
G1: identifiable divergence/ancestry and visible phenotype history? Otherwise **HISTORY_UNRESOLVED**.
G2: floral molecule/pathway function actually measured? Otherwise **COMPETENCE_UNOBSERVED**.
G3: intrinsic re-expression vs immigration vs introgression discriminated with calibrated validation? Otherwise **MECHANISM_NOT_IDENTIFIABLE**.
G4: independent angiosperm replication? Otherwise no cross-clade generalization.
