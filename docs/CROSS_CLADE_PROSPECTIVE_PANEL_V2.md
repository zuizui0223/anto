# Prospective angiosperm comparison panel v2

## Study identity
**Question:** Does a plant lineage's colonization history transport a hidden capacity to reuse a previously unexpressed reproductive trait? The biological mover is the **plant lineage, seed or allele**, not the pollinator as default protagonist.

**Do not conflate:** 1) visible coloured-region occupancy; 2) intrinsic re-expression by a previously white lineage; 3) pollen-mediated introgression; 4) new mutational reconstruction; 5) phenotypic plasticity. Geography alone does not establish which route occurred.

## Selection before trait-outcome analyses
This is a **candidate and evidence-role registry**, NOT a pooled empirical dataset or confirmation of reversals in all named clades.

| Independent clade | Positive evidence already available | Current role | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|---|

**No pooled phylogenetic inference from the above row count.** Petunia's two mechanistic outcomes share a genus; published taxa and populations are not independent experimental replicates.

## What would establish generality
Freeze inclusion criteria *before* accessing outcomes:
1. Public provenance for tip/population geography, dated tree distributions, tissue-scoped pigment phenotype; matched genotype/functional evidence for competence-aware subset.
2. At least two additional **independent** plant clades beyond Cirsium and Petunia with reliable historical orientation and capability data; otherwise call results *replicated within selected systems*, not universal.
3. Compare an M0 visible/geography-only model with M1 explicit geographic transitions, M2 latent retained/reduced competence, M3 pollen/seed reticulation. Include no-gene-flow, no-decay and plasticity nulls.
4. Hold out an **entire clade or geographically independent lineage group**, never split images of the same plant across training/test. Score held-out calibrated likelihood and prediction of a mechanistically validated recolouring event.
5. When latent competence lacks independent measurement, stop at geography and colour history; never infer a global competence map from photographs alone.
6. Controls for taxon concept, time-scale, phylogenetic uncertainty, source-photo sampling, whole-organ vs petal-specific pigmentation, diploidy/cytotype, founder history, environmental shifts and gene-flow history.

## Key alternative hypotheses
- PLEIOTROPY: persistent use of an anthocyanin toolkit outside the petals reduces degradation, maintaining potential after white floral expression.
- DISPERSAL-ORDER: migrating before vs after loss of competence changes accessible states when region-specific degradation differs.
- FOUNDER: small founding populations stochastically lose or retain variants independent of selection by pollinators.
- RETICULATION: geographic reacquisition can be produced by pollen alleles when intrinsic re-expression has decayed.
- NULL: visible colour and geography are sufficient to predict events at available temporal and sampling resolution; no predictive gain from molecular competence.

Do not assume any universal monotonic "window", single flower-colour phenotype scale or common effect across pigmentation systems. Other reproductive traits require trait-specific molecular/functional competence definitions and separate prospective tests.
