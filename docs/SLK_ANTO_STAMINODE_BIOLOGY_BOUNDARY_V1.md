# A biological evidence constraint for SLK × ANTO: staminode structure is not ancestral function

**21 October 2026 | cross-angiosperm biological reference, no new molecular or population-genetic model and no original experiment.** The actual original-source numerical staminode F2 reanalysis is owned in [SLK draft PR #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121), not duplicated into ANTO or Cirsium aza3.

## Why this case matters to trait evolutionary afterlife

A biologically strong, previously published example goes beyond visible pigment gain/loss: **stamens can lose pollen-production identity while still contributing a derivative organ scaffold**, and that scaffold can support distinct pollination functions or no detected function depending on the visitor. This does not mean the original anther fertility function was restored.

**Walker-Larsen & Harder 2001**, *Evolution* 55:477–487, DOI 10.1111/j.0014-3820.2001.tb00782.x, removed modified sterile stamens (staminodes) in four *Penstemon* species: 
- *P. ellipticus* (bee): nectar barrier increased handling/contact and stigma pollen receipt
- *P. palmeri* (bee): mechanical stigma-contact lever; removal reduced pollen receipt but not attraction or visit duration
- *P. centranthifolius* and *P. rostriflorus* (hummingbird): no effect of removal detected on measured pollination outcomes

Their phylogenetic/developmental account suggests staminodes disappeared visibly and later reappeared in Cheloneae, but **no source-matched native genetic retention/regain rates were directly measured**. Staminodes do not produce the ancestral pollen as functional anthers.

**Johns et al. 2024**, *EvoDevo* 15:6, DOI 10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3, studied the reverse evolutionary organ-identity transformation in *Aquilegia jonesii*, which has lost canonical staminodes and produces stamens instead. Their experimental F2 cross with *A. coerulea* 'Origami' and original QTL analysis implicate **9 independent QTL**, with subtraits and two whorls partly distinguishable (authors' reported genetics). We have independently recomputed **real source phenotype combinations** from their CC BY 4.0 800-row Supplement 3 CSV: 231 phenotyped plants, 400 same-flower inner/outer pairs; 99 flowers have outer anthers without inner anthers, zero reverse; 181 inner whorls show more lateral expansion than outer versus two reverse; 187/800 whorl-score rows simultaneously show anther presence and strong lateral expansion plus interwhorl fusion (129/400 flowers; 91/231 plants). The fusion score is **the same flower-level interwhorl property printed on both whorl rows**, not 800 independent fusion states.

Morphological mixing is **not** demonstration of three independently retained functional genetic modules. Their anther presence code did not assay pollen viability, and the observed configurations emerged in an artificial F2 cross. They cannot be used as natural rates of ancestral trait restoration or as measures of ecological fitness. The published QTL results are prior original authors' analyses and do not arise from refitting this 800-row phenotype-only file.

**Ortega-Olivencia et al. 2016**, *International Journal of Plant Sciences* 177:671–681, DOI 10.1086/687994, found that large staminodes assist reproductive outcomes after repeated natural pollinator visits in *Scrophularia lyrata* and *S. scorodonia* but no comparable effect was detected in small-staminode *S. canina*, where the organ is naturally absent in 10.5–20% of flowers. This provides an additional context-dependent function and natural phenotypic presence/absence anchor, **not** a direct molecular regained-capacity estimate.

## ANTO theory implication and its remaining falsifier

**There are at least three separable biological outcomes for stamen-derived structures**:
1. **Original function lost:** the organ no longer serves as a fertile pollen-producing stamen.
2. **Derivative scaffold retained/reappears with new function:** a sterile staminode helps visitors contact stigma or regulates access; its actual function depends on pollinator and organ position.
3. **Fertile stamen identity reappears:** a clade such as *Aquilegia jonesii* changes formerly staminodial whorls to stamens with partial persistence of staminode-like filament morphology; developmental components are not one binary whole-organ switch.

The same visible structural *homology* is not the same ancestral *functional identity*. It is therefore incorrect to count adaptive staminodes, fertile stamen regain and visible colour reacquisition as equivalent ANTO return events. The strongest combined prediction is that the genetic/developmental ability to reconstruct an **organ component** and the ecological benefit of its **pollen and mechanical functions** can diverge. Testing this in one organism requires both source-resolved native heritable components and independent matched pollinator/payoff manipulations, not a collage of unrelated clades.

This is **not a claim that staminode functional reinvention is a new discovery**: Walker-Larsen & Harder 2000–2001 and later direct experiments established it. This document provides an audit-friendly counterexample to conflating phenotype/structure/function in ANTO's theory and supplies a potentially fruitful joint-organ experiment with SLK. Source-reanalysis code, evidence receipt and its independent CI exist only in SLK PR #121. Cirsium specimen-level biology continues exclusively in aza3.

## 21 October 2026: further original-data checks and actual conflict in Parnassia

The SLK [empirical morphology PR #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121) now adds an independent **same F2 genotype, two-flower** source audit rather than inferring capacity from single morphology scores. Across 169 F2 plants with flowers a/b, the AN-positive, LE≥2, fused mosaic appeared in **both** flowers for 38 plants, only flower a for 23, only flower b for 16, and in neither for 92. If one records only flower a, **61/169 (36.1%)** plants display the mosaic; recording either of two flowers finds **77/169 (45.6%)**. Among 108 flower-a-negative plants, 16 display the mosaic in flower b. This is not an intrinsic evolutionary regain rate: the **same F2 plant** shares its genotype, developmental stage differences and microenvironment, while 169 plants have two observations each. Structural mosaics were much more reproducible within a genotype than matching morphological marginals from unrelated plants, although never perfectly so; F2 individual-level anther/fusion repeats are not biological replicates of independent allele gain.

**ANTO lesson:** One flower that is negative for a *morphological mosaic* does not establish that the same F2 plant cannot develop that mosaic. This is an observational distinction between morphological **penetrance** and confirmed loss of developmental **competence**, NOT molecular evidence that an ancestrally missing function has been restored. In Supplement 3, fusion is a between-whorl trait repeated on two rows of the same flower, not two independent capacities. A simple LE score difference of one plant from the paper's prose (138 vs 139 same-inner-LE F2 plants) remains disclosed.

**Independent real SLK-style opponent interaction:** Wen et al. (2024), *Ecology and Evolution*, DOI [10.1002/ece3.70380](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.70380), experimentally clipped the glossy nectar-mimicking staminode tips or the full organ in *Parnassia wightiana*, finding reductions in prospective pollinator behavior and seed production. Florivorous *Nonarthra variabilis* beetles preferred the staminodes over ovaries and chewed tips first; ovary protection through decoy consumption is a **hypothesis**, not a measured net benefit. This is the same floral-derived organ class expressing both mutualist and antagonist attraction, not another ANTO genetic-regain event. The authors state raw data at Dryad DOI **10.5061/dryad.b2rbnzsqg**, but the linked public Dryad endpoint was **not available** at this verification; numbers reported for that paper are original published summaries, not our downloaded-individual reanalysis.

**Integrate with anto main v11/v12 without claim inflation:** ANTO v11 distinguishes restored ancestral pigmentation from new-pathway coloration; v12 shows in a conditional competing-risk model that first recovery *route* can shift toward an alternative while **total recovery capacity decreases**. The developmental analogue here is conceptual only: counting an AN-positive organ, a sterile pollinator lever, and a protective carpel sheath all as "ancestral pollen function regained" confounds distinct outcomes. No source F2 genotype tracking, ancestral module assay or genetically inherited natural regain was completed; one must not fit the v12 route rates or ecological v6 switching parameters from these phenotypic counts.

**Cross-paper hypothesis, NOT result:** A modified stamen-derived organ could persist for visual/structural protection or partner handling despite loss of its original reproductive function, creating retained morphology that is available for later remodelling. The test requires the same individual/lineage's ancestral and derived organ functionality, co-retained necessary genomic components, source-resolved allele histories and actual full reproductive fitness. Parnassia gives the functional-conflict positive control; Aquilegia gives the mosaic developmental state positive control; neither alone proves their causal coupling.

### A same-species molecular reference exists, but it is not the missing staminode assay

Tang et al. (published online 28 November 2025, *BMC Plant Biology* 26:4, 2026), DOI [10.1186/s12870-025-07646-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-025-07646-9), additionally studied **Parnassia wightiana** using whole-tissue insecticidal extracts, metabolites, and **shoot/flower** RNA-seq (GSA accession **CRA028314** reported in the published Data Availability section). The authors report 87 TPS-family members in their annotated transcriptomic study; roots were excluded from RNA-seq because of extraction challenges, although their root extracts were highest in insecticidal activity against *Mythimna separata* larvae.

This is useful as a potential *species-level* sequence resource to identify candidate genes and tissues in a later organ-specific study. **It is neither the staminode-specific expression nor the same plant genotypes in Wen et al. 2024**; RNA of whole flowers and shoots does not localize a retained native functional program to staminode apices, and insect toxicity toward moth larvae does not prove defense against the florivorous beetle *Nonarthra*. We did NOT download/reanalyse this new RNA dataset, and its GSA accession is a published source claim, not a verified local sequence inventory.

The best falsifiable *within-species* ecological question now becomes whether glossy staminodes attract beetles but also redirect them away from more consequential female tissues, and whether biochemical/structural differences among staminodes, filaments and ovaries change the actual ovule-level fitness cost. This requires organ-specific chemistry/transcriptomes and a beetle × floral-organ factorial experiment on matched identifiable plants. **Do not equate insecticidal compound abundance in roots with ancestral stamen developmental retention.**

## 21 October v3: actual positional constraints on a structurally possible return

The original Johns et al. 2024 `Aquilegia` F2 phenotype-only dataset was independently re-enumerated in [SLK PR #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121), now under a [v3 source-replay contract](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/blob/biology/empirical-staminode-organ-afterlife-20261021/docs/SLK_ANTO_AQUILEGIA_POSITIONAL_NESTEDNESS_V3.md).

Of 400 same-flower inner/outer whorl pairs, anthers occurred in the inner whorl in 184 flowers and outer whorl in 283, with **zero** cases of inner AN present but outer absent (the converse had **99** cases). Under original 0–3 filament lateral-expansion scores, **323** flowers scored `LE_inner≥2` and **243** `LE_outer≥2`, with exactly **one** case of outer high but inner low (plant 132, flower a). These two **opposing** positional containment rules coexist in **399/400 flowers**. Replacing the 400 repeated flowers with a first-scored-flower-only sample of **231 F2 plants** yields **230/231** both-rule consistency. Lower and higher expansion thresholds `LE≥1` and `LE≥3` yield one and zero reversed cases, respectively, documenting rather than hiding threshold sensitivity.

**Biological meaning:** a partial restored shape is not a free independent combination of anther production and lateral laminar expansion across positions. Developmental position constrains available morphologies, while earlier v2 same-genotype 2-flower data show incomplete penetrance. A *morphological* shape observed in a hybrid F2 is neither evidence of return along a natural historical route nor functional restoration of the ancestral pollen-producing capacity.

The original 2024 authors already reported the fading developmental boundary and whorl-specific/polygenic QTL effects; this project claims **no first discovery of spatial order, no new QTL refit, no genetic independence test and no empirical ANTO rate**. Its use is to limit the otherwise-too-permissive feasible candidate set in a possible SLK–ANTO interface. A direct test would spatially manipulate and genotype regulatory programs within one floral lineage, assay pollen fertility and secondary staminode function, and evaluate complete reproductive fitness under different partner contexts. No such manipulation or multi-generational natural reversal has been observed here.

An independently source-audited but *different anatomical comparison* exists in [aza3 *Cirsium* morphology](https://github.com/zuizui0223/aza3/blob/main/docs/CIRSIUM_TWO_RADIATIONS_HEAD_GEOMETRY_MOSAIC_20261021.md): four coarse head orientation×bract posture combinations among 63 North American taxa. This does NOT establish a shared position rule or genetic accessibility rate between *Cirsium* and *Aquilegia*.

## 21 October v6 — original published genetic data support a *partly* separable architecture, not a freely reversible organ

The [SLK Aquilegia empirical draft #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121) has now archived the original **Johns et al. (2024)** [QTL Table 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3/tables/1) and independently calculated its trait/whorl 1.5-LOD interval intersections. This QTL table is a **published genotype-association SUMMARY**, not individual raw genotypes; we have NOT refitted the original marker calls, checked causal genes or analyzed new plants.

The 16 published trait-specific QTL lines derive from **nine independent QTL** reported by the original authors. Among detected same-chromosome QTL comparisons:
- **Anther inner vs outer:** all 3 of 3 corresponding support-region pairs overlap (Chr2, Chr3, Chr5).
- **Filament lateral expansion inner vs outer:** none of 3 corresponding pairs on Chr2/Chr3 overlap at 1.5-LOD intervals.
- **Filament lateral expansion outer vs interwhorl fusion:** both 2 of 2 corresponding pairs overlap (Chr2, Chr3); inner expansion vs fusion has 0 of 3 such overlaps.

In the observed F2 **phenotype data**, by contrast, AN-positive forms appear in the outer whorl before they appear in the inner in the **spatial containment** sense (0 inner-only vs 99 outer-only flowers), and LE≥2 appears almost invariably first in the inner whorl (only one outer-only among 400). The true combined conclusion is **strong phenotype-level architectural containment with partly separated detected genetic regions**. This is consistent with positional developmental programs and incomplete organ reassembly, not unrestricted independent Lego components. Within-genotype flowers can also differ, preventing one negative flower from demonstrating lost capacity.

**Important distinction for ANTO's theory:** a chromosome interval missing from one trait's 1.5-LOD range does NOT mean the corresponding gene module vanished; a shared range does NOT identify pleiotropy versus linkage; and anther presence does NOT demonstrate ancestral pollen-fertility recovery. The original 2024 study's 329/334 usable marker genotypes and nine-QTL interpretation belong to the AUTHORS.

The source manuscript names [jasonwjohns/aquilegia_staminodes](https://github.com/jasonwjohns/aquilegia_staminodes) as its code repository, but its public main tree as of this audit contains **only a 57-byte README** (one public commit). The original processed 329×599 QTL matrix is not presently available there. ENA BioProject PRJNA1089234 holds FASTQ sample aliases for **all 231 phenotyped F2 plants**, but those aliases alone are not validated allele calls. Downloading ~49 GB before asking for the processed matrix would add cost without resolving the key biological function question.

SLK alone owns the original-source phenotype/QTL re-tabulation, reproducible tests and provenance in `docs/SLK_ANTO_AQUILEGIA_GENETIC_PARTIAL_COUPLING_V6.md`. ANTO does **not** duplicate the F2 data or claim measured native reacquisition rates. The combined SLK × ANTO fitness-capacity hypothesis remains experimentally untested because pollinator handling, pollen viability, antagonistic injury and total seed fitness were not measured on these genetic lines. The central generalizable question is which partially co-maintained functions of a *homologous* organ can remain selectable under new partner environments, not whether a source QTL reanalysis alone confirms evolutionary afterlife.

## 22 October 2026: reproduction by a surviving organ is NOT the return of a lost genetic organ

A key *real* ecological comparison now joins separate verified source studies without pretending they measure ANTO's genetic return rate.

**Parnassia wightiana** (Wen et al. 2024, DOI [10.1002/ece3.70380](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.70380)): glossy sterile-stamen-derived staminodes improve pollinator interaction and seed counts in the authors' removal experiment, while *Nonarthra variabilis* beetles prefer eating the same organ. But the strongest beetle-infested site A lacked a surviving contemporaneous beetle-free comparison; beetles chewed flower peduncles/labels, and the later-season comparison also changes phenological stage and collected-fruit loss. The authors' indicated Dryad `doi:10.5061/dryad.b2rbnzsqg`, internal id **139500**, currently returns `Identifier cannot be viewed` through the public API; no individual-pair net-fitness reanalysis or proof of protective decoy effect is available in this project.

**Cirsium undulatum** (West & Louda 2021 DOI [10.1002/ajb2.1744](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajb2.1744)): the original USDA CC0 106-mother plant data contain directly co-measured seed production at apical heads and lateral heads. A new source-authenticated anatomical-route audit in [aza3 PR #76](https://github.com/zuizui0223/aza3/pull/76) classifies 105 internally consistent mothers (one 2007 source plant's regional seed sum is irreconcilable). In 2008 under apical damage/ambient herbivory, **7/12** maternal plants produced good seed **only from lateral heads**, while **5/12** made none. Under insecticide/damaged apex, **11/19** still made positive seed at the apical head, showing damage does not mean total removal. Intact apices with ≥1 lateral good seed improved from **2/15 ambient to 14/21 insecticide** (exact Fisher p=.00218, eight-contrast BH q=.0174); apical-damage association under ambient increased 2/15 to 7/12 (nominal p=.0369 but BH q=.1477, **not robust after eight comparisons**).

These are **positive, contemporaneous reproductive-route observations** and context-dependent enemy exposure, not anything resembling a previously lost gene being preserved or spontaneously reactivated. ANTO's subject—same-lineage molecular/functional competence retained across evolutionary absence—still needs allele-level and multigenerational observations. Likewise the *Aquilegia* F2 mosaics on this branch are hybrid morphological segregation, not native wild trait regain. No *Cirsium* raw data or independent rerun is duplicated in ANTO; the original study remains owned by aza3, with a [separate SLK biological synthesis](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/blob/biology/empirical-staminode-organ-afterlife-20261021/docs/SLK_ANTO_REAL_ECOLOGICAL_FUNCTION_AND_SPATIAL_FALLBACK_20261022.md).

**Conceptual resolution for a future joint test:** organismal anatomical fallback (one existing flower head succeeding when another fails), developmental potential for new organ configurations, and heritable evolutionary return of a lost ancestral functional component are **three different endpoints**. Their ecological selective payoffs can only be compared where pollen delivery, antagonists, seed counts, and the same functional module history are actually measured in the same lineages.

## 23 October — observed anatomical coverage is a different endpoint from retriable ancestral recovery

Source-faithful [aza3 draft PR #84](https://github.com/zuizui0223/aza3/pull/84) uses genuine USDA *Cirsium undulatum* original maternal counts to compute `P(A∨L)` where A is at least one good seed from an existing apical head and L at least one good seed from existing lateral heads. In 2008 damaged-apex + insecticide treatment, **11/19** mothers yielded apical seeds, **15/19** lateral seeds and **19/19** at least one route; four mothers succeeded only apically and eight only laterally. Under damaged apex + ambient insects, **7/12** succeeded laterally and **5/12** had both routes fail. The observed extra paired coverage over the better single marginal route is **4/19** vs **0/12** respectively. These quantities are directly verified from plants (original 105 internally coherent maternal records), but their ratios have **no genetic afterlife interpretation**.

This makes the conceptual firewall for ANTO main v14 particularly important. In ANTO v14 a latent ancestral or alternative phenotype can generate **new heritably viable descendants** through specified repeated attempts under theoretical loss/activation/demographic hazards. In USDA *Cirsium*, apical/lateral heads were already present **within the same mother** during a season, and good seed presence is not even measured seedling establishment. Neither the v14 repeated-restoration probabilities nor the prior v12 ancestral-vs-alternative recovery rates can be estimated from observed spatial seed-route coverage.

A biologically important prediction can still be drawn **for future testing, not claimed as evidence**: if a derivative structural module persists because it performs a useful alternate ecological function, it may keep certain ancestral developmental components available for future redeployment. To verify, use the SAME lineage's source-identified DNA function, floral organ induction, alternate-organ counterfactual whole-lifetime fitness and offspring transmission over generations. Real same-season anatomical insurance is not such a test.

The related original 2022 *C. arvense* source analysed in merged aza3 #83 shows a bagging-associated decline in head viable-seed conversion, not a measured anto gene loss or reactivation. Cross-taxon patterns cannot be multiplied into one observed selective-return probability. All Cirsium code stays in aza3; ANTO owns only this scope-aware biological interpretation.
