# A biological evidence constraint for SLK × ANTO: staminode structure is not ancestral function

**21 October 2026 | cross-angiosperm biological reference, no new molecular or population-genetic model and no original experiment.** The actual original-source numerical staminode F2 reanalysis is owned in [SLK draft PR #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121), not duplicated into ANTO or Cirsium aza3.

## Why this case matters to trait evolutionary afterlife

A biologically strong, previously published example goes beyond visible pigment gain/loss: **stamens can lose pollen-production identity while still contributing a derivative organ scaffold**, and that scaffold can support distinct pollination functions or no detected function depending on the visitor. This does not mean the original anther fertility function was restored.

**Walker-Larsen & Harder 2001**, *Evolution* 55:477–487, DOI 10.1111/j.0014-3820.2001.tb00782.x, removed modified sterile stamens (staminodes) in four *Penstemon* species: 
- *P. ellipticus* (bee): nectar barrier increased handling/contact and stigma pollen receipt
- *P. palmeri* (bee): mechanical stigma-contact lever; removal reduced pollen receipt but not attraction or visit duration
- *P. centranthifolius* and *P. rostriflorus* (hummingbird): no effect of removal detected on measured pollination outcomes

Their phylogenetic/developmental account suggests staminodes disappeared visibly and later reappeared in Cheloneae, but **no source-matched native genetic retention/regain rates were directly measured**. Staminodes do not produce the ancestral pollen as functional anthers.

**Johns et al. 2024**, *EvoDevo* 15:6, DOI 10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3, studied the reverse evolutionary organ-identity transformation in *Aquilegia jonesii*, which has lost canonical staminodes and produces stamens instead. Their experimental F2 cross with *A. coerulea* 'Origami' and original QTL analysis implicate **9 independent QTL**, with subtraits and two whorls partly distinguishable (authors' reported genetics). We have independently recomputed **real source phenotype combinations** from their CC BY 4.0 800-row Supplement 3 CSV: 231 phenotyped plants, 400 same-flower inner/outer pairs; 99 flowers have outer anthers without inner anthers, zero reverse; 181 inner whorls show more lateral expansion than outer versus two reverse; 187/800 whorl-score rows simultaneously show anther presence and strong lateral expansion plus interwhorl fusion (129/400 flowers; 91/231 plants). The fusion score is **the same flower-level interwhorl property printed on both whorl rows**, not 800 independent fusion states.

Morphological mixing is **not** demonstration of three independently retained functional genetic modules. Their anther presence code did not assay pollen viability, and the observed configurations emerged in an artificial F2 cross. They cannot be used as natural rates of ancestral trait restoration or as measures of ecological fitness. The published QTL results are prior original authors' analyses and do not arise from refitting this 800-row phenotype-only file.

**Ortega-Olivencia et al. 2016**, *International Journal of Plant Sciences* 177:671–681, DOI 10.1086/687994, found that large staminodes assist reproductive outcomes after repeated natural pollinator visits in *Scrophularia lyrata* and *S. scorodonia* but no comparable effect was detected in small-staminode *S. canina*, where the organ is naturally absent in 10.5–20% of flowers. This provides an additional context-dependent function and natural phenotypic presence/absence anchor, **not** a direct molecular regained-capacity estimate.

## ANTO theory implication and its remaining falsifier

**There are at least three separable biological outcomes for stamen-derived structures**:
1. **Original function lost:** the organ no longer serves as a fertile pollen-producing stamen.
2. **Derivative scaffold retained/reappears with new function:** a sterile staminode helps visitors contact stigma or regulates access; its actual function depends on pollinator and organ position.
3. **Fertile stamen identity reappears:** a clade such as *Aquilegia jonesii* changes formerly staminodial whorls to stamens with partial persistence of staminode-like filament morphology; developmental components are not one binary whole-organ switch.

The same visible structural *homology* is not the same ancestral *functional identity*. It is therefore incorrect to count adaptive staminodes, fertile stamen regain and visible colour reacquisition as equivalent ANTO return events. The strongest combined prediction is that the genetic/developmental ability to reconstruct an **organ component** and the ecological benefit of its **pollen and mechanical functions** can diverge. Testing this in one organism requires both source-resolved native heritable components and independent matched pollinator/payoff manipulations, not a collage of unrelated clades.

This is **not a claim that staminode functional reinvention is a new discovery**: Walker-Larsen & Harder 2000–2001 and later direct experiments established it. This document provides an audit-friendly counterexample to conflating phenotype/structure/function in ANTO's theory and supplies a potentially fruitful joint-organ experiment with SLK. Source-reanalysis code, evidence receipt and its independent CI exist only in SLK PR #121. Cirsium specimen-level biology continues exclusively in aza3.
