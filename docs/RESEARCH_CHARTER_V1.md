# anto research charter v1 — from Ryukyu thistles to general plant evolutionary biogeography

## Why this question emerged
The *Cirsium brevicaule* (white) and *C. irumtiense* (coloured) contrast in aza3 poses a historical puzzle. Do not impose a white-to-coloured ancestor-descendant order from geography, and do not treat the taxon names as guaranteed distinct species. Evaluate competing ancestry/taxonomic hypotheses and uncertain ancestral states.

## Central hypothesis
Visible geographic ranges of reproductive traits can diverge from the geographic ranges of latent capacities to produce them. Plant propagule movement, lineage sorting, local loss, regulatory degradation and introgression jointly shape that divergence.

## Prospectively falsifiable predictions
P1: Among white-flowered lineages, independently assayed pathway competence differs beyond what is explained by visible colour and phylogenetic proximity alone.
P2: Where loss of expression is dated independently, pathway competence tends to decay with time **conditional on** tissue-level pleiotropic use, effective population size and gene flow; there is no assumed universal monotonic clock.
P3: Other-organ pathway use predicts longer floral reacquisition competence after accounting for ancestry and ecological context. Correlation alone does not establish pleiotropic constraint.
P4: Region-level reappearance of colour can occur without within-lineage re-expression, through dispersal or introgression.
P5: Adding independently measured competence improves preregistered out-of-sample prediction of establishment of locally re-expressed phenotypes over visible-state-only and geography-only baselines. A failure is informative.

## Four nested scales
A. Local Ryukyu population ancestry, phenotype and pigment/flower regulation; identify mechanisms rather than presuppose them.
B. Replicated white/coloured contrasts in *Cirsium* and other independent angiosperm clades.
C. Global biogeography of visible floral transitions with explicit limits: public colour records alone do not identify latent capacity.
D. Trait-general extension after validation: nectar guides, rewards and floral/head architecture; each requires its own developmental evidence.

## Minimum data table
individual_id; voucher_id; taxon_concept; population_id; coarse_region; sampling_date; calibrated_flower_colour; floral_pigment; floral_RNA_stage; nonfloral_pigment_expression; nuclear_ancestry; organellar_marker; cytotype; hybridization_evidence; dated_tree_version; colonization_history_scenario; evidence_grade; provenance.

## Confounders and safeguards
Species delimitation, environmental plasticity, anthesis stage, pigments other than anthocyanins, founder effects, ancestral polymorphism, incomplete lineage sorting, introgression, sampling/observer bias, taxon-specific expression, and inaccurate molecular orthology. Keep geographic coordinates deidentified when sensitive.

## Decision gates
G0: independent molecular or functional competence measurements available? If not, report visible-history inference only.
G1: can ancestral retention, re-expression, immigration and introgression be separated by simulation-based identifiability tests? If not, label NOT_IDENTIFIABLE.
G2: do independent clades replicate a prespecified directional prediction? If not, no universal claim.
G3: does competence improve held-out predictions with proper baselines? If not, no predictive advantage claim.

## Publication aim
Main biological claim: history-dependent dispersal of latent reproductive capacity explains geographic patterns that visible traits alone cannot.
Alternative outcome: visible geography cannot identify latent evolutionary capacity, and the work derives the minimum observations needed to discriminate mechanisms.
